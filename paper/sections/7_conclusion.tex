\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied how to induce the classification dimensions that legal instruments
and academic taxonomies use for crypto-assets, and proposed \sys, which reduces
each source to its definitional sections, extracts term types in one pass over
the whole corpus held in a cached context, verifies every citation, and orders
the term types into a DAG by one cited is-a query per node. On 24 documents from
four jurisdictions, \sys produced 93 term types with 317 values, 97.9\% of whose
480 citations verify, recorded a cross-source conflict for 69 of them, and
linked them by \BEdges\ weighted is-a edges under one root per category, with
the information gain, depth and leaf-use curves a maintenance stage can steer
by. Future work follows the
limits of this evidence: (i) expert validation of whether the verified
citations support their term types, with annotator agreement; (ii) repeated
runs and a second model to measure the stability of the catalogue, whose
identifiers are not yet stable across runs; (iii) auditing the is-a edges, whose
weights are coarse and whose citations verify less often than the term types',
and placing terms under the term types; and (iv) using the catalogue as the label space for classifying Web-sourced token
descriptions.
