\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We studied how to induce the classification dimensions that legal instruments
and academic taxonomies use for crypto-assets, and proposed \sys, which reduces
each source to its definitional sections, extracts term types in one pass over
the whole corpus held in a cached context, and verifies every citation. On 24
documents from four jurisdictions, \sys produced 93 term types with 317 values,
97.9\% of whose 480 citations verify, and recorded a cross-source conflict for
69 of them, in one call costing well under a dollar. Future work follows the
limits of this evidence: (i) expert validation of whether the verified
citations support their term types, with annotator agreement; (ii) repeated
runs and a second model to measure the stability of the catalogue, whose
identifiers are not yet stable across runs; (iii) ordering the term types into
a hierarchy and placing terms under them, for which a first run exists; and
(iv) using the catalogue as the label space for classifying Web-sourced token
descriptions.
