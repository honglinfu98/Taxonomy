Crypto-assets are issued, traded and described on the Web, and the vocabulary
that governs them is published as Web documents by regulators, standard-setters
and researchers who do not share a dictionary. The same token is a crypto-asset
under the EU's MiCA regulation, a digital asset under US law and a virtual asset
under the FATF standards, and each definition discriminates along dimensions
that the text never names. Manually built taxonomies capture one perspective at
a time and go stale as instruments change, while LLM methods for taxonomy
construction and ontology learning assume a fixed label space or a general-domain
benchmark and do not trace their output to a legal source. In this paper we
propose \sys, a framework that induces the classification dimensions, which we
call term types, from the definitions themselves. \sys (1) builds a
definitions-first corpus from 24 legal instruments and academic taxonomies,
(2) extracts term types in a single pass over the whole corpus held in a cached
context, requiring a document, section and verbatim quote for every dimension
and value, and (3) verifies every quote against the source. On a corpus spanning
the EU, the UK, the US and six international bodies, \sys produces 93 term types
with 317 values and 480 citations, 97.9\% of which verify, and records a
cross-source disagreement for 69 of them, in one five-minute call to a small
