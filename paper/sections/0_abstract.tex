Crypto-assets are issued, traded and described on the Web, and the vocabulary
that governs them is published as Web documents by regulators, standard-setters
and researchers who do not share a dictionary: the same token is a crypto-asset
under MiCA, a digital asset under US law and a virtual asset under FATF, and
each definition discriminates along dimensions the text never names. Manual
taxonomies capture one perspective and go stale, while LLM taxonomy and
ontology-learning methods assume a fixed label space and do not trace their
output to a legal source. In this paper we
propose \sys, a framework that induces the classification dimensions, which we
call term types, from the definitions themselves. \sys (1) builds a
definitions-first corpus from 24 legal instruments and academic taxonomies,
(2) extracts term types in a single pass over the whole corpus held in a cached
context, requiring a document, section and verbatim quote for every dimension
and value, (3) verifies every quote against the source, (4) orders the term types into a directed acyclic graph from one cited
is-a distribution per term type, and (5) uses the graph as the label
space for Web content, grows it only where tagged content raises its
information gain, and (6) indexes the tagged content on the graph for
retrieval and navigation by the same gain. On a corpus spanning the EU, the UK, the
US and six international bodies, \sys produces 93 term types with 317 values and
480 citations, 97.9\% of which verify, and links them by 279 weighted is-a
edges for a few dollars of API calls. \NIterCap\ maintenance iterations tag the whole on-topic DLT-Corpus of
\AllItems\ papers and patents, verify \EvidMin--\EvidMax\% of tag evidence and
grow the graph by \AllAddedTypes\ dimensions and \AllAddedValues\ values for
\$\AllCost; dimensions discovered in one iteration are among the most used
in the next. The index answers queries
over \RItems\ items in microseconds, \RSpeedMin\ to \RSpeedMax\ times faster
than a flat scan, and facet gain reaches ten items in \RGainSteps\ questions. \todo{One sentence of comparative evidence once RQ5--RQ7 are run.} The
