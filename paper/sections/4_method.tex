\label{sec:method}

\sys has six modules (\cref{fig:overview}), described in data-flow order:
corpus construction, extraction, verification, hierarchy, maintenance and
retrieval.

\subsection{Definitions-First Corpus Construction}
\label{sec:method:corpus}

We do not give the model whole statutes: a regulation such as \src{MiCA}
defines its vocabulary in a few articles and spends the rest on obligations
that refer back to them, so we take the definitions articles, glossaries,
classification conditions and business-model descriptions and nothing else. \Cref{tab:corpus} lists the result: 21 legal
instruments from the EU, the UK, the US and six international bodies, plus two
academic taxonomies of blockchains~\cite{tasca2019taxonomy} and of
crypto-assets~\cite{zhang2026cryptotaxonomy} and one paper describing a
domain corpus~\cite{hernandez2026dltcorpus}, with reference lists and large
tables removed.

Each PDF is converted to text and cut by line ranges or regular-expression
markers specified once per document; US bills need the layout-preserving mode,
page footers are dropped, and the Senate print of the \src{CLARITY Act}, which
reproduces the House text, contributes only the substitute's definitions and
its network-token section. The corpus is one XML-tagged text file in which every document carries an
integer index and metadata and is split into \texttt{<section label="...">}
blocks, so a citation can name a document and a section unambiguously. The file
holds 32 sections and 97.8 thousand words, which the model's tokeniser counts
as 235,298 tokens.

\subsection{The Extraction Task}
\label{sec:method:task}


The prompts are given verbatim in \cref{app:prompts}. The system instructions
define the objects of \cref{sec:problem} in the model's terms and state the grounding and quality
rules; they precede the corpus and never change, so the cache survives every
task. The user prompt states the scope (assets, DLT systems, actors, activities,
obligations or risks), gives the JSON schema, asks for ordering by category
and grounding count, and declares the corpus the only source of truth.

\textbf{Manifest.} Our first run cited section labels from memory and got
them wrong in 9\% of citations, with 65 term types. We therefore append a
\emph{manifest}: one line per document listing its index, title and every
exact section label, with the instruction that no other labels exist. The
manifest is generated from the corpus at run time, so it cannot drift from the
file the model sees.

\subsection{One Call over a Cached Corpus}
\label{sec:method:run}

We place the whole corpus in the system prompt, after the stable instructions
and behind a prompt-cache breakpoint~\cite{gim2024promptcache} with a one-hour lifetime; the task prompt
is the user message. Every later task, and every prompt iteration, then pays
only for the small uncached part. The choice is also methodological:
recognising that \src{MiCA}'s ``referencing the value of one official
currency'' and the \src{GENIUS Act}'s ``fixed amount of monetary value'' answer
the same question needs both passages in view, which a retriever would have to
anticipate. The cost of a run is therefore linear in the
corpus size, with the corpus priced at the cache-read rate and only the task
prompt and the output priced in full; we report measurements in
\cref{sec:exp:rq1,sec:exp:rq2}.

A single streamed call produces the output. We disable adaptive thinking, which on
a first attempt spent more than 100,000 tokens without emitting text. We parse the JSON and, if a response is cut off by the output limit,
salvage every complete term type and flag the result as truncated.

\subsection{Citation Verification}
\label{sec:method:verify}

A quote may be invented, compressed or attached to the wrong section, so we
check all of them. For each citation we
look up the document and section, normalise both the quote and the section text
to lower-case alphanumerics, and accept the quote if it is a substring. If it is
not, we split the quote into 12-character shingles and accept it when at least
80\% of them occur inside one window of the section twice the quote's length.
The window tolerates footnote markers and PDF spacing and rejects a paraphrase
stitched from distant phrases. When a quote fails under the cited label but
passes under another label of the same document, we \emph{relabel} it,
correcting the section, keeping the model's label alongside, and counting the
citation as verified. Everything else is rejected; every citation carries a
verified flag, and the rejection rate measures desideratum~(D1). Verification establishes that the quote exists where the
model says it does; it does not establish that the quote supports the
dimension, a limit we return to in \cref{sec:exp:rq1}.

\subsection{From Term Types to a Hierarchy}
\label{sec:method:hierarchy}

\textbf{One question per node.} With the catalogue $\mathcal{Y}$ in hand we ask
the model, for each term type $x$, how probable it is that $x$ is-a $y$ for every
other term type $y$ in the same category, plus \emph{none}, meaning $x$ is a
top-level dimension. One user message per $x$ carries the definition of is-a
from \cref{sec:problem}, the full record of $x$ and the candidate parents by
identifier, name and question. The model returns at most $k{=}6$ entries, each
with a probability, a rationale of at most thirty words, and citations in the
format of Task~A; the top entry, unless it is \emph{none}, must cite at least
one passage. The corpus and a second cached block holding the compact
catalogue are read from cache, so each call sends only the record of $x$ and
its candidates.

\textbf{Normalisation.} We drop parents that are not in the candidate set, merge
duplicates, remove the \emph{none} entry while keeping its mass as $p_{\text{none}}(x)$
for reference, and renormalise the remaining weights to sum to one. Citations
are verified exactly as in \cref{sec:method:verify}.

\textbf{Consolidation into a DAG.} Every weighted edge is a candidate. We take
edges in decreasing weight and keep each one unless the parent already descends
from the child, which would close a cycle; a mutual pair $x \to y$, $y \to x$
thus resolves to the stronger direction, and an exact tie resolves by identifier
order and is reported. No weight threshold is applied; a display threshold
can hide weak edges later. The first kept edge of a node is its primary parent,
which gives a tree view.

\textbf{Metrics for maintenance.} We attach to the DAG three measures of how
well it discriminates. At a node $y$ with children $C(y)$ the kept edge weights define a
branching distribution
\begin{equation}
  q_y(c) = \frac{w(c \to y)}{\sum_{c' \in C(y)} w(c' \to y)}, \qquad c \in C(y),
  \label{eq:branch}
\end{equation}
and a \emph{random descent} starts at a uniformly chosen root and follows $q_y$
until it reaches a leaf. The \emph{information gain} at $y$ is the entropy
$\entropy(q_y)$ in bits, zero for leaves and single-child nodes; we report its
sum over nodes and its expectation along a descent, the bits a full walk
resolves. With $F(d)$ the probability that a descent has reached a leaf by depth
$d$ and $D$ the maximum depth,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{AUC} = \frac{1}{D}\int_0^D F(d)\,\mathrm{d}d,
  \qquad
  \text{effective leaves} = 2^{\entropy(r)},
  \label{eq:auc}
\end{equation}
where $r$ is the distribution of the leaf a descent ends at. AUC is one when
every root is a leaf and falls as branches run deeper; effective leaves counts
how many leaves descents use. We compute all three overall, per
category, and for the sub-DAG obtained at every display threshold from 0 to 1,
which is the sensitivity study of \cref{sec:exp:rq2}.

\subsection{The DAG as a Label Space and Its Maintenance}
\label{sec:method:maintain}

\textbf{Tagging (Task C).} The model receives one cached system block, the
\emph{catalogue}: every term type with its question, its values as codes with
definitions, and its is-a parents with weights. The Part~1 corpus is not sent.
For each content item, a title and abstract, it returns tags $(y, v, c, e)$: a
term type, a value code, a confidence, and an evidence span of at most twenty
words copied from the item; optional proposals of a value or term type the
catalogue lacks; and an off-topic flag. We reject unknown identifiers, verify
every evidence span against the item with the matcher of
\cref{sec:method:verify}, and record the is-a ancestors of each tagged term
type as implied.

\textbf{Label space.} Tagging places an item on node $y$ and, by is-a, on every
ancestor. For node $y$ let $S(y)$ be the items tagged directly at $y$, $C(y)$
its closure, $S(y)$ plus the closure of its children, and $R(y) \subseteq C(y)$
the \emph{residual}, items on no child of $y$. From these we compute per node
the entropy of the value distribution of $S(y)$ (impurity of the node's own
split), its dominance (share of the top value), the \emph{branching gain}, the
entropy of the split of $C(y)$ over its children plus the residual, and the
residual fraction $|R(y)|/|C(y)|$. A random descent now starts at a root with
probability proportional to its closure and at $y$ stops with the residual
share or moves to a child in proportion to its closure, which gives the
objectives of \cref{eq:branch,eq:auc} from content rather than from the prior
weights; both are reported.

\textbf{Proposals and discovery (Task E).} The measurements name \emph{dormant}
nodes ($C(y) = \emptyset$), \emph{collapsed} nodes (dominance $\ge 0.85$ with at
least ten items), the highest-gain nodes, and \emph{discovery targets}, nodes
with at least five residual items making up at least 30\% of the closure. For
each target the model sees the node, its values and children, the tagger's
seeds and up to forty residual items with evidence spans, and proposes at most
four candidates, new values of $y$ or new child term types with two to six
values, each with supporting items and one evidence span. Because the items shown are a sample of the residual, the share of shown items
that support a candidate is extrapolated to the whole residual. A candidate is
accepted when at least five of the shown items support it, when making it a
child that captures its estimated support would raise the branching gain at
$y$ by at least 0.1 bit, and when its evidence span is found verbatim in a
supporting item. Dormant and collapsed nodes are reported, not
removed.

\textbf{Evolution.} One model call consolidates the accepted candidates into
version $n{+}1$, merging candidates that ask the same question at different
nodes, placing each new term type under the node it refines and dropping
duplicate values; additions are marked as candidates with edges at weight one
until the is-a query of \cref{sec:method:hierarchy} re-estimates them. The
seed is never modified, batches are disjoint, and every iteration appends its
sample, tags, objectives, additions, cost and time to a log.

\subsection{Retrieval over the DAG}
\label{sec:method:retrieval}

\textbf{Index.} The tagged items of every iteration are placed on the current
version of $G$ and numbered $0..N{-}1$, and every posting list is a bitset, so
set algebra on postings is one integer operation. Node $y$ carries $S(y)$, one
bitset per value $v \in V(y)$, and its closure $C(y)$, the union of $S$ over
its descendants; each category carries the union of its nodes. From $G$ we
precompute, in topological order, the descendants and ancestors of every node,
the best is-a path strength $s(d, a)$, the maximum over paths from $d$ to $a$
of the product of edge weights, the lowest common ancestors of any node set,
and the information content $\mathrm{IC}(y) = -\log_2 |C(y)|/N$.

\textbf{Queries.} An atom denotes the subtree $C(y)$, the direct items $S(y)$,
the items with value $v$ at $y$, the descendants $d$ of $y$ with
$s(d, y) \ge \sigma$, or a category; atoms combine with AND, OR and NOT. An
item's score is the sum over the positive atoms of its match strength, the tag
confidence for a direct or value match and $\max_d c(d)\,s(d, y)$ over its
tagged descendants $d$ for a subtree match, so an item reached only by weak
edges ranks below one tagged at $y$ itself. The lowest common ancestors of the query nodes are
offered as the generalisation of the query; when fewer than $k$ items match,
items from the nodes most similar to the query nodes, by
$\max_c s(a, c)\,s(b, c)$ over common ancestors $c$, are appended and marked.

\textbf{Facets.} For a result set of $n$ items and a node $y$ it touches, the
child subtrees of $y$ with the residual, and the values of $y$ with the
untagged items, are the questions a user could be asked next. Picking option
$o$ with probability proportional to its size $m_o$, the expected narrowing is
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:facet}
  \mathcal{N}(y) = \sum_{o} \frac{m_o}{\sum_{o'} m_{o'}} \log_2 \frac{n}{m_o},
\end{equation}
zero for an option that covers the whole set. The facet with the highest
$\mathcal{N}$ is the best next question, which is the branching gain of
\cref{sec:method:maintain} evaluated on a result set instead of a batch: the
same objective that grows the DAG routes a search through it.
