\section{Problem Formulation}
\label{sec:problem}

\textbf{Corpus.} A corpus $\mathcal{D} = \set{d_1,\dots,d_N}$ is a set of source
documents, each with metadata (type, jurisdiction, year) and partitioned into
labelled sections $d_i = \set{s_{i1},\dots,s_{in_i}}$. Documents are
\emph{legal instruments}, of which we keep the definitions articles,
glossaries, classification conditions and business-model descriptions, or
\emph{taxonomy frameworks}, academic papers that propose classification
dimensions.

\textbf{Term and term type.} A \emph{term} is a word or phrase that some section
defines or classifies, such as \term{e-money token} or \term{permissioned
ledger}. A \emph{term type} $y$ is a question along which definitions
discriminate, such as \ttype{reference asset} or \ttype{consensus mechanism},
together with a set of \emph{values} $V(y)$, the categories a thing can take on
that dimension. A term type is \emph{grounded} in $\mathcal{D}$ when at least one
section uses it as a criterion, or defines several terms that differ along it.
A \emph{citation} is a triple $(i, \ell, q)$ naming a document index, an exact
section label and a verbatim quote $q \subseteq s_{i\ell}$ of at most thirty
words.

\textbf{Problem (term type induction).} Given $\mathcal{D}$, produce a catalogue
$\mathcal{Y} = \set{(y, V(y), C(y), A(y), \eta(y))}$ in which every term type $y$
and every value $v \in V(y)$ carries at least one citation in $C(y)$, $A(y)$
lists the names other sources use for $y$, and $\eta(y)$ records where sources
disagree on $V(y)$ or on its thresholds, citing each side. No seed taxonomy, gold
labels or training pairs are given.

\textbf{Problem (is-a hierarchy).} Term type $x$ \emph{is-a} term type $y$ when
$y$ is a more general dimension of which $x$ is a specialisation: everything $x$
classifies is also classified by $y$, $x$ refines $y$'s question, and the values
of $x$ could sit beneath one or more values of $y$. Given $\mathcal{Y}$, produce a
weighted DAG $G = (\mathcal{Y}, E, w)$ with $w(x \to y) \in (0, 1]$ an estimate of
$P(x \text{ is-a } y)$, every edge cited, and no cycle. A node may have several
parents, which is what makes $G$ a DAG rather than a tree.

\textbf{Problem (maintenance).} Given $G$ and a batch of content items, each
tagged with pairs $(y, v)$, $v \in V(y)$, and placed by is-a on every ancestor
of $y$, measure how much a descent of $G$ discriminates the content and, at
nodes whose items stop short of every child, propose new values of $y$ or new
children of $y$, accepting a candidate only when it raises that measure and
is supported by verified evidence. Additions are marked as candidates; the seed is
never edited.

\textbf{Problem (retrieval).} Given $G$, the tagged items and a query, a
Boolean combination of nodes, values and subtrees of $G$, return the items it
denotes, ranked by how strongly each is a kind of the queried nodes along the
weighted is-a edges, together with the question, a child split or a value
split of $G$, whose answer would narrow the result most.

\textbf{Desiderata.}
\begin{enumerate*}[label=(D\arabic*)]
  \item \emph{faithfulness}: every citation resolves to a passage that exists
    where it is said to exist;
  \item \emph{coverage}: every document that grounds a dimension is represented;
  \item \emph{consistency}: one dimension appears once, with aliases, and
    conflicting values are kept with their jurisdiction rather than resolved;
  \item \emph{cost}: induction over the full corpus fits a stated token budget.
\end{enumerate*}

\textbf{Design principles.} No gold catalogue of term types exists for any
legal corpus, and building one is the annotation we want to reduce, so an LLM
extracts and the effort moves to the input, restricted to definitional text,
and to the output, constrained to cite and checked mechanically. Faithfulness
ranks above recall: an ungrounded dimension is dropped and an unfound quote
stays flagged.
