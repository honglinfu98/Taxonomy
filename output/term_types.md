**Grounded in** docs [4, 13, 17, 20, 21, 22, 8] (EU, US, UK, INTL). Aliases: peg type, reference value, stabilisation reference, reference asset.

The corpus distinguishes stablecoins by the asset or basket they are designed to track, which drives classification as e-money token, asset-referenced token, or payment stablecoin. Sources differ on whether a single official currency, a basket, a commodity, or another crypto-asset counts as a valid reference.

- **basket or other assets/rights**: Stablecoin referencing another value or right, or a combination including one or more official currencies, classed as asset-referenced token. [doc 4, Articles 2-3: Scope and Definitions]

*Notes:* MiCA splits by single official currency (EMT) vs other references (ART); the GENIUS Act and UK SI 2026/102 use fiat-currency references only, and the UK excludes assets referencing non-fiat assets even if priced in fiat. - **non-deterministic (probabilistic)**: Agreement eventually settles with decreasing probability of disagreement, e.g. Bitcoin forks. [doc 1, Body: Sections I-XII (introduction through conclusion)]

*Notes:* Basel uses settlement finality as a legal test; the taxonomy uses it as a technical property. The two are not identical.

**Grounded in** docs [1] (INTL). Aliases: block storage, state model.

- **repo / secured funding**: Token minted by locking collateral and redeemed by burning, resembling a repurchase agreement. [doc 2, Sections 1-5: motivation, related work, methodology, taxonomy dimensions, dataclass and decision rules, mapping results (summary)]
- **capitalising share class**: Value accrual with constant quantity and rising redemption ratio, resembling a fund share class. [doc 2, Sections 1-5: motivation, related work, methodology, taxonomy dimensions, dataclass and decision rules, mapping results (summary)]
- **voting equity share**: Governance token analogous to voting equity. [doc 2, Sections 1-5: motivation, related work, methodology, taxonomy dimensions, dataclass and decision rules, mapping results (summary)]

*Notes:* Analogies are descriptive, not legal; the taxonomy states its TradFi mapping is non-exclusive and jurisdiction-sensitive.
